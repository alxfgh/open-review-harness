# Stopping distance of delivery vans on wet asphalt

## Abstract
We measured stopping distances for delivery vans on wet asphalt and show that a safe following distance can be derived from speed alone.

## Methods
Twelve identical vans were braked from a constant test speed of 30 m/s on a wet test track at 15 degrees Celsius, with new tyres and full load. Stopping distance was measured with a calibrated laser system. Each van completed ten runs.

## Results
Mean stopping distance was 92 metres (standard deviation 4 metres). The test speed of 30 m/s corresponds to 180 km/h. Mean deceleration, calculated from the kinematic relation between speed and distance, was 4.9 metres per second squared.

## Discussion
Because the test speed of 180 km/h is far above the limit on delivery routes, our distances are a conservative upper bound for ordinary driving, and fleets can use them without adjustment for urban routes.

## Conclusion
A following distance of 92 metres is safe for delivery vans on wet asphalt at all legal speeds.
