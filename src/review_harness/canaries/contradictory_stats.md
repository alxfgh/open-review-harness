# A daily mindfulness app for reducing clinic missed appointments: a randomised trial

## Abstract
In a randomised trial of 600 adults, a daily mindfulness app significantly reduced missed clinic appointments compared with usual care (odds ratio 0.85, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.20, p = 0.003).

## Methods
Adults with at least one chronic condition were randomised one to one to the app or to usual care, using a computer-generated sequence with allocation concealment. The primary outcome was any missed appointment over six months, taken from clinic records by assessors unaware of allocation. We estimated odds ratios by logistic regression, adjusted for age and sex, with two-sided tests at the 0.05 level.

## Results
Of 300 participants in each group, 96 in the app group and 107 in the usual care group missed at least one appointment. Baseline characteristics were similar between groups. The adjusted odds ratio for a missed appointment was 0.85 (95% CI 0.60 to 1.20, p = 0.003). Adherence to the app averaged 4.2 days per week.

## Discussion
The intervention produced a statistically significant reduction in missed appointments. Clinics should consider offering mindfulness apps routinely. Limitations include a single health system and the relatively short follow-up.

## Conclusion
A daily mindfulness app significantly reduces missed appointments.
