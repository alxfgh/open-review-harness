# Sleep duration and reaction time in shift workers: a cross-sectional survey

## Abstract
We surveyed 250 hospital shift workers on sleep duration and psychomotor reaction time. Workers sleeping under six hours were slower by 34 ms on average.

## Methods
Participants were recruited from three hospitals between March and June. Night-shift workers were compared with day-shift workers. Reaction time was measured using a standard ten-minute vigilance task on a laptop, and sleep duration was self-reported using a seven-day diary. Differences in means were tested using two-sided t-tests with a significance level of 0.05.

Mean reaction time was 312 ms among those sleeping under six hours (n = 102) and 278 ms among those sleeping six hours or more (n = 148), a difference of 34 ms (p = 0.004).

## Discussion
Short sleep was associated with slower reactions in this sample. The design is cross-sectional, so causation cannot be inferred. Self-reported sleep may be subject to recall bias, and shift pattern may confound the association.

## Conclusion
Hospitals should consider reaction-time screening for staff reporting short sleep.
